# Provenance and publication scope

Prepared 17 November 2026 by Claudio Mattioni with AI assistance.

This deposit is a curated snapshot for the paper *A homogeneous mono-unstable
polyhedron with fourteen vertices*, version 1.0. It is not an archive of the
entire development repository.

The integer coordinates and two original checking paths were retained from
the unpublished development snapshot 94a1f1e. The new manuscript, independent
integer checker, contraction certificate and packaging workflow were prepared
after that snapshot. The old local tag v1.0 does not identify these new files.
CHECKSUMS_C.sha256 identifies the material included here; text is normalized to LF.

Former Theorems A (pentahedra) and B (a lower bound of nine vertices) were removed
from this publication's claims after comparison with prior public material,
including the Humeniuk research archive. Their source, certificates and history
remain in the development repository, outside this deposit.

The current paper explicitly cites the eighteen-vertex construction in
Balancing polyhedra, subsequent lattice work, and the public sixteen-vertex
construction at commit cf21399a241e48a1ca872b20846be46f6a7b8025 of
shuhualin1023/mono-unstable-16. The latter was rechecked with exact arithmetic
during preparation and has (V,E,F) = (16,30,16) and (S,H,U) = (2,1,1).

Among the inspected sources, no homogeneous mono-unstable example with fourteen
or fewer vertices was found. This is a bounded literature-search conclusion,
not a guarantee of worldwide priority. Spirals and moving projected centroids
are prior ideas; the concrete fourteen-vertex variable-thickness realization
and its exact verification are the subject of this paper.

The reserved DOI is 10.5281/zenodo.24286668. The record remained unpublished
during preparation, so version 1.0 denotes the intended first public release.
The author's private repository, local outreach drafts, previous archives,
downloaded tools, A/B certificates and third-party Reshetov data are excluded.

The original searches and development used Claude (Anthropic); restructuring,
additional checks and the contraction certificate used Codex (OpenAI).
Neither AI-assisted checks nor the term “independent checker” imply an
independent human review.
